Most enterprise AI fails as an operating model, not as technology.

What we think

Most initiatives get scoped as a technology purchase and land as one. The model goes into the existing workflow, the workflow keeps its old assumptions, and the pilot posts a modest number that nobody can defend in a second budget cycle.

The question isn't which model. It's what the work becomes when you assume the model from day one, and whether your people are fluent enough to run it. Anyone can buy the capability. Almost nobody builds the fluency.

Human-centered design didn't stop being the differentiator. It became the only one left.

How we work

Human-centered, held to its own standard.

  1. 01

    Start with the people doing the work

    We study the real workflow first: the handoffs, the workarounds, the decisions people actually make. Before anyone picks a model.

  2. 02

    Prototype the new workflow, not the model

    We redraw the process assuming the capability, then test it with the people who will run it. Cheap to change, fast to learn.

  3. 03

    Measure adoption, not demos

    We track whether the work changed: who uses it, what got faster, which decisions got better. A demo is not a result.
